# CapyBudget User Guide

**Stay chill with your money.**

CapyBudget helps you record everyday spending, plan savings, manage business invoices, and see when money might become tight. This guide covers the features available in the current MVP—the first working version of the app.

You do not need accounting or technical experience. Instructions use the English button names. If your app is in Bahasa Indonesia, use the menu translation table below or tap **ID** to switch to English.

Examples use Indonesian rupiah (IDR). All example names and amounts are fictional.

## 1. Start here

### Create your account

1. Choose **Get started free** or open the sign-up page.
2. Enter the requested name, email address, and password.
3. Submit the form and check your email for the verification code.
4. Enter the code on the email verification screen.
5. Complete the welcome setup.

If the email has not arrived, check spam and confirm the address you entered. Use the resend option when it becomes available, then use the newest code.

If you forget your password later, choose **Forgot password** from the login page and follow the email instructions. Email verification codes and authenticator codes serve different purposes; see [account security](#18-protect-your-account-and-data).

### Complete the welcome setup

The setup walks you through four steps:

1. **How will you use CapyBudget?** Choose Personal, Business, or Both. Enter your business name when requested.
2. **Currency and language.** Choose your main currency, such as `IDR`, and English or Bahasa Indonesia.
3. **Your first wallet.** Give it a name, choose its type, and enter the money already there as its starting balance.
4. **Budget or goal.** Create your first monthly budget or savings goal, or choose Skip for now.

Choose **Take me to my dashboard** when finished. If you selected Both, check the workspace name before adding wallets or transactions; each workspace has its own records.

**Starting balance example:** If your bank contains Rp2,000,000 when you begin tracking, enter `2000000`. Do not also add that same amount as new income unless it really arrived after your starting point.

## 3. Read your dashboard

The Dashboard brings together your recorded balances, income, expenses, recent transactions, budget progress, savings goals, and upcoming bills. Assistant information may be unavailable until you enable it and provide enough data.

- **Balance:** money shown by your starting balances and recorded activity. It is not a live bank balance.
- **Income:** money received, such as salary or a recorded customer payment.
- **Expenses:** money spent, such as food, rent, or supplies.
- **Net change:** income minus expenses for the period shown. Transfers between your wallets do not count as earnings or spending.
- **Safe to spend:** an estimate from the Assistant after planned outflows and protected money. It can be lower than your balance.
- **Budget and goal progress:** how much you have used or saved.

Start by checking the workspace and any displayed period. Open the relevant page to see the records behind a number.

Green indicates income or positive progress, coral indicates expenses or a limit being exceeded, blue indicates savings or information, and yellow indicates a gentle caution. Read the labels as well as the colors.

## 4. Set up accounts and wallets

**What this does:** Keeps balances separate so you can see where your money is.

1. Open **Accounts**.
2. Find **Add an account**.
3. Enter a recognizable name, such as “Main bank” or “Cash wallet.”
4. Select Cash, Bank, E-wallet, Credit card, Savings, or Investment wallet.
5. Enter the opening balance and choose **Save**.
6. Repeat for your other wallets in this workspace.

Adding a bank or card account does not connect it to your bank. You enter its activity yourself. An investment wallet records money; it does not fetch stock or crypto prices.

Use **Rename** to change a wallet's name. Use **Archive** when you stop using it; historical entries remain available. Read any message shown if the app cannot archive it yet.

Use the workspace's currency consistently. Automatic currency conversion is not included in this version.

## 5. Record income and expenses

### Quick add for everyday entries

**What this does:** Lets you record a simple transaction with fewer steps.

1. Open **Quick add** from the navigation.
2. Choose **Expense** or **Income**.
3. Check the displayed wallet and currency.
4. Enter the amount and choose a category.
5. Add a note if useful, then choose **Save**.
6. Wait for the saved confirmation before closing or adding another entry.

For a Rp45,000 lunch, choose Expense, your payment wallet, amount `45000`, and Food and dining. Plain digits without currency symbols are a simple way to enter whole amounts.

Quick add is intended for today's entries. Use **Open full form (date, tags, receipts)** for more detail or a different date.

### Add a detailed transaction

1. Open **Transactions** and choose **New transaction**.
2. Choose Income or Expense.
3. Select the account and category.
4. Enter the amount and date.
5. Add notes, a merchant name, and tags as needed.
6. Choose **Save transaction**.

A merchant is the shop, person, or organization involved. Using consistent merchant names helps the Assistant learn your category choices.

### Attach a receipt or document

1. Save the transaction first.
2. Find it in Transactions and use **Attach receipt** in its available actions.
3. Select a JPEG, PNG, WebP, or PDF file.
4. Wait for the receipt confirmation. Use its file link to download it later.

Each transaction supports up to five files, each no larger than 10 MiB (about 10 MB). Attachments are stored with the entry; the app does not read the receipt and fill in the amount automatically.

## 6. Record transfers

**What this does:** Records money moving between two of your own wallets without inflating income or expenses.

1. Open transaction entry and choose **Transfer**.
2. Select the source account and **To account**.
3. Enter the amount and, in the full form, the date and an optional note.
4. Save the transfer.

**Example:** You withdraw Rp200,000 from your bank into cash. Record one transfer from Main bank to Cash wallet. Do not record a separate expense and income for that same movement.

CapyBudget records the movement; you still make the actual transfer or withdrawal outside the app. Both wallets must be in the selected workspace and use a supported matching currency. Do not expect one transfer to move records between personal and business workspaces.

## 7. Organize categories and tags

**What this does:** Helps you understand what money was used for and find related entries later.

- **Category:** the main purpose, such as Food, Salary, or Supplies.
- **Subcategory:** a more specific group inside a category, such as Coffee inside Food.
- **Tag:** an extra label that can cross categories, such as Holiday or Client project.

### Create a category or subcategory

1. Open **Categories**.
2. Enter a category name and choose Income or Expense.
3. For a subcategory, select a **Parent category** of the same type. Otherwise leave Top-level category selected.
4. Choose **Add category**.

### Create and use a tag

1. On Categories, enter a **New tag** and choose **Add tag**.
2. Open a detailed transaction and select the tag under **Tags (optional)**.
3. Save the transaction. Open the tag dropdown and choose one or more tags. Selected tags have a check mark.

Use Rename or Archive to organize existing categories and tags. Archiving keeps their past transaction history.

## 8. Find and correct transactions

### Search, filter, and sort

1. Open **Transactions**.
2. Use **Search notes or merchant** to find a word or business name.
3. Open **Filters** to narrow by transaction type, dates, account, category, tag, or minimum/maximum amount.
4. Choose a sort option: Date, Amount, Category, or Tag.
5. Choose **Apply**.

Use **Clear all** to remove filters. Use **Load more** when additional results are available. If something seems missing, check the selected workspace and filters first.

### Fix or remove an entry

- Choose **Edit**, correct the details, and select **Save changes**.
- Choose **Delete** and confirm to remove an incorrect entry and reverse its effect on the recorded balance.
- Use **Show recently deleted**, then **Restore**, to bring back an available deleted entry.

If an entry came from a recorded invoice payment, use the invoice's payment correction controls when directed. That keeps the invoice and income record consistent.

## 9. Set up recurring transactions

**What this does:** Creates a schedule for regular salary, rent, subscriptions, loan payments, or transfers.

1. Open **Recurring** and find **Add a recurring rule**.
2. Name it, such as “Monthly salary.”
3. Choose the transaction type, amount, account, and category—or destination for a transfer.
4. Choose Daily, Weekly, Monthly, or Yearly, then enter **Every**. For example, Weekly with Every set to 2 means every two weeks.
5. Set the first date and an optional end date.
6. Choose **Posting behavior** and save the rule.

| Posting behavior | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Ask me to confirm | The entry waits for you to confirm it on or after its due date. |
| Automatically record | The app records the scheduled entry when due. It does not make a bank payment. |

Review **Upcoming occurrences**. Confirm an entry that happened or Skip one that did not. You can edit, Pause, Resume, or Archive a rule from the rules list.

For dates such as the 31st, a shorter month uses its last available day. Later months follow the original schedule again.

A recurring entry and a bill reminder can describe the same payment. Use the bill's matching controls, explained below, so the forecast can recognize the connection. Do not manually add another expense if the recurring entry already recorded it.

## 10. Make a budget

**What this does:** Sets a weekly or monthly spending limit for an expense category.

1. Open **Budgets** and find **Add budget**.
2. Enter a name, such as “Food this month.”
3. Choose the expense category.
4. Enter the amount and select Weekly or Monthly.
5. Choose the start date and review the alert thresholds.
6. Save.

A monthly budget follows a calendar month; a weekly budget follows a calendar week starting Monday.

**Example:** With a Rp1,000,000 Food budget and Rp600,000 recorded spending, you have used 60% and have Rp400,000 left. Spending in a category's subcategories is included where that parent category is budgeted.

The pond bar and labels show how much is used. As spending rises, the color moves from cool blue toward yellow and coral. A negative amount left means you are above the plan; it does not stop you recording transactions.

An 80% alert means the app lets you know when spending reaches four-fifths of the limit. Each threshold alerts once per budget period. Correcting an expense can resolve an alert, but does not restart that threshold's alert for the same period.

Budgets do not reserve money or move it into a separate account. Archive a budget when you no longer need it.

## 11. Track a savings goal

**What this does:** Tracks progress toward something you want to save for.

1. Open **Goals** and find **Add savings goal**.
2. Enter a name, such as “Emergency fund.”
3. Set the target amount and an optional target date.
4. Optionally choose a linked account.
5. Save the goal.
6. On its card, choose **Add savings** from the Action field, enter a contribution amount, and choose **Save**.

The blue pool fills as recorded contributions grow. Use **Withdraw** to record a reduction in the goal's savings and review **Contribution history** to see changes.

**A goal contribution is a record of money set aside.** It does not transfer cash, reduce your bank balance, or automatically match the linked account's balance. If you move money between wallets, record that separately as a transfer, then update the goal. Do not record saving as an expense merely because you moved it between your own wallets.

## 12. Manage bills and due dates

**What this does:** Reminds you about money you need to pay, such as rent, electricity, or a subscription.

1. Open **Bills** and find **Add bill**. Bills with exactly the same name and due-date day are grouped together, even when created separately. For example, SPayLater due on 02-11-2026 and 02-12-2026 appears in one group. Expand a group heading to see its dates and manage individual bills.
2. Enter its name, amount, and first due date.
3. Choose One time, Weekly, Monthly, or Yearly.
4. Set how many days before the due date you want a reminder.
5. Optionally enter **Number of bills** (1–300). The first due date counts as the first bill. Leave it empty for an ongoing recurring schedule; a one-time bill always creates one due date.
6. Save, then review the upcoming bill cards.

For example, a weekly schedule with a count of **4** creates bills on the starting date and one, two, and three weeks later. Each occurrence can be paid or skipped separately.

### After you pay

1. Pay the bill through your normal bank or payment service.
2. Record the expense in Transactions, unless a recurring entry has already recorded it.
3. On the bill, open **Forecast and payment matching**.
4. Choose its **Payment account** and **Link existing payment transaction**.
5. Choose **Save assumptions**. Linking the payment also marks that occurrence paid.

If you only want to acknowledge payment without linking a transaction, choose **Mark paid** and confirm instead. **Mark paid does not create an expense.** Linking the existing transaction helps the forecast avoid counting the payment twice. The matching list shows compatible transactions, so check the amount and account if yours is missing.

Use **Matching recurring expense** when a recurring rule represents the same bill. Use Skip for an occurrence you will not pay, Reopen to correct a paid/skipped state, and Archive bill to stop using that bill schedule.

### Give the forecast better payment dates

In **Forecast and payment matching**, you can record an Expected payment date. Only enter **Flexible until** when the biller actually allows a delay. Choose **Save assumptions**.

These dates help Capy estimate cashflow. They do not renegotiate the bill or change its contractual due date.

Sharing forecast settings uses the same exact name and due-date day as the display group, even when bills were created separately. It includes unpaid one-time bills and future bills that match. Paid, skipped, linked-payment, and archived bills are excluded.

To share settings, open an unpaid bill, set its forecast assumptions, select **Apply to all unpaid bills in this group**, then choose **Save for bill group**. This replaces the payment account and date assumptions on unpaid bills and saves defaults for future occurrences. Payment dates follow each bill’s due date: a payment expected three days after November’s due date is also expected three days after December’s due date. Paid or skipped bills and recorded transactions are not copied or changed. Leave the checkbox off to edit only the selected bill’s account and date assumptions; the recurring expense link belongs to the selected bill’s original schedule and is not copied to other schedules. Shared date offsets are limited to 365 days.


## 13. Keep business finances separate

**What this does:** Gives each business its own records while keeping one login.

1. Open the workspace selector and choose **Add business** below the separator.
2. Enter the business name. Choose a currency from the searchable list (type its name or code, such as IDR), then choose a time zone (search for a city or region, such as Jakarta). A time zone tells the app which local day to use; for example, `Asia/Jakarta`.
3. Choose **Create**.
4. Switch between personal and business workspaces using the workspace selector.

Always check the selected workspace before adding a transaction. The Dashboard, wallets, categories, invoices, reports, and Assistant use that workspace's records.

### Complete the business profile

1. Select the business workspace and open **Business profile**.
2. Enter the legal name, business email, address, and other relevant contact details.
3. Add your tax ID and business logo if needed.
4. Review any financial-year fields and choose **Save business profile**.

These details appear on invoices. The app requires the legal name, email, and address before issuing an invoice. A logo may be PNG, JPEG, or WebP, up to 2 MiB.

## 14. Create and manage invoices

**What this does:** Creates a document asking a customer to pay your business and tracks what has been received.

### Create and send an invoice

1. Select the correct business workspace and open **Invoices**.
2. Choose **New invoice**.
3. Enter the customer's name, email, and relevant address details.
4. Set the issue date and due date.
5. Add each item or service with its description, quantity, and unit price.
6. Enter any applicable discount or tax rate, plus notes and payment instructions.
7. Choose **Save draft** and review the calculated totals.
8. Use **Edit draft** for corrections. When ready, choose **Issue invoice**.
9. Choose **Download PDF** to save a copy, or **Send invoice** to send it by email.

The tax field calculates the rate you enter; it does not decide which taxes apply. Customer details belong to this invoice; a separate customer directory is not part of the MVP.

### Understand invoice states

| State | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Draft | Still being prepared. |
| Issued | Finalized for use; not necessarily emailed. |
| Sent | The app has recorded the send state. Check delivery history if there is a problem; this does not prove the customer read it. |
| Partially paid | Some money has been recorded, with an amount still outstanding. |
| Paid | The full amount has been recorded as received. |
| Overdue | Money remains outstanding after the due date. |
| Void | The invoice has been cancelled through the app's controls. |

### Record money received

1. Open the invoice after the customer pays you.
2. Choose **Record payment**.
3. Enter the received amount and date, receiving account, income category, and an optional reference.
4. Save and check the outstanding amount.

**Recording an invoice payment creates an income transaction.** Do not add the same receipt again in Transactions. A partial payment leaves the rest outstanding. The app will not let you record more than the remaining amount.

If a payment was recorded incorrectly, use **Reverse payment**, give the reason, and then record the correct payment. For cancellation, use **Void invoice** and follow the displayed checks and confirmation.

If a customer pays late, review the invoice and any follow-up suggestion in Assistant. Update collection expectations where the invoice provides them so Capy has realistic dates.

## 15. Use Capy, your cashflow assistant

**What this does:** Estimates future money available and suggests actions based on your records. Cashflow simply means money coming in and going out over time.

Capy's current tools use calculations and category rules that learn from your choices. This version does not provide a free-form AI chat, and it does not send financial data to an external AI provider. Suggestions are informational, not professional financial, tax, or legal advice.

### Enable forecasts and choose the data

1. Open **Assistant**.
2. Find **Privacy and data controls**.
3. Enable local forecasts and, if wanted, proactive alerts and category learning.
4. Choose the sources Capy may use: history, recurring transactions, bills, invoices where relevant, and other displayed sources.
5. Review **Accounts in forecasts**. Choose the accounts to include, their low-balance thresholds, and protected amounts.
6. Set an additional safety buffer if wanted, then choose **Save privacy settings**.

Protected amounts and the safety buffer are money Capy leaves out of its safe-to-spend estimate. They do not lock funds at your bank. Forecast account choices focus on spendable wallets rather than treating credit limits or investments as available cash.

### Read a 30-, 60-, or 90-day forecast

1. Choose the forecast period and account scope.
2. Review the chart and **Lowest projected balance**.
3. Check any shortfall or low-balance date.
4. Open daily values and included events to understand the estimate.

**Expected timing** includes expected incoming money. **Conservative timing** is more cautious about money not yet received. Forecasts account for planned outflows before relying on incoming funds.

An overdue invoice without a revised collection date may be listed separately instead of being assumed to arrive immediately.

### Understand “Safe to spend today”

This estimate considers the selected period, planned outflows, and protected money. It is not your total balance and is not a promise that every bill will be affordable.

If it shows **Unavailable**, read **Forecast limits**. Common reasons include insufficient history, excluded sources, bills without payment accounts, or payments that may have been counted twice. The history calculation needs at least 28 completed days for the selected account; it does not necessarily require a transaction on every day.

Correct the records or data choices and review the updated forecast. You can exclude an unusual one-time transaction from the historical baseline using the displayed control; its actual transaction and account balance remain unchanged.

### Act on a suggestion

1. Read the suggestion and its **Why** explanation.
2. Open the related bill, invoice, or goal to review the facts.
3. If a Prepare action is offered, open its preview.
4. Review the exact details and confirm only if they are right.

| Suggestion | What confirmation does |
| --- | --- |
| Invoice reminder | Queues the displayed reminder for the customer. |
| Goal contribution | Records a savings allocation; no bank money moves. |
| Flexible payment date | Updates forecast timing; it does not change the contractual due date or pay the bill. |

You can also mark a suggestion Helpful or Not helpful, **Snooze 7 days**, or Dismiss. If an action has expired or its source changed, refresh and review a new preview.

### Teach category suggestions

1. Enable **Learn merchant categories from your corrections** and allow merchant names in the data controls.
2. In detailed transaction entry, use a consistent merchant name and the correct category.
3. Save similar entries and correct wrong categories when needed.
4. After enough consistent examples, look for **Suggested category** and choose **Use suggestion** if it fits.

Learning generally needs at least three examples with strong agreement. It may offer no suggestion when uncertain. Review **Learned category rules** to disable a rule or reset learning. Rules belong to you and the current workspace.

You can also download or delete Assistant data from its controls. Deleting Assistant data does not delete your financial transactions or invoices.

## 16. Read and export reports

**What this does:** Turns recorded activity into summaries you can review or share.

1. Open **Reports** in the correct workspace.
2. Choose one of the available reporting periods.
3. Read the totals and charts, then use the detailed tables when you need exact figures.

| Report or chart | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Income and expense bars | How money received compares with money spent. |
| Expense pie chart | How spending is divided among categories. |
| Balance line/trend charts | How balances or net changes vary across the period. |
| Cashflow statement | Opening cash, money moving in/out, and closing cash. |
| Budget vs actual | The planned limit compared with recorded spending. |

Transfers between included cash accounts are not new income or expenses. A transfer crossing the report's cash-account boundary may appear as a cash movement. Read the report labels when reconciling balances.

Where **Classify cashflow categories** is shown, assign the appropriate type of activity if needed: everyday operations, investment activity, or funding such as loans. Ask the person who keeps your accounts if you are unsure which applies.

### Download PDF, Excel, or CSV

1. Choose the workspace and reporting period first.
2. Select **PDF**, **Excel**, or **CSV**.
3. Wait for the report to be prepared and download it.
4. Use saved report history to download an available earlier snapshot again.

PDF is useful for reading and sharing, Excel for spreadsheets, and CSV for a simple table that other programs can open. A saved report is a snapshot of the records when it was made. Create a new report after corrections if you need updated figures.

Exports contain real amounts even when privacy mode hides them on screen. These reports summarize recorded activity; business profit-and-loss, balance-sheet, and tax-ready reporting are later features.

## 17. Manage notifications

**What this does:** Brings due dates and changing money conditions to your attention.

Open the bell for a quick view or **Notifications** for the full list. Open the related record, mark a notice read, snooze it where offered, or dismiss it. Marking a notice read does not mark a bill paid or change any money.

| Alert | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| Bill, invoice, or payment due | The date and whether payment was actually made or received. |
| Budget threshold | Spending compared with the category's limit. |
| Recorded low balance | A wallet's current recorded balance against your chosen limit. |
| Projected low balance/shortfall | A future risk calculated by Assistant. |
| Unusual spending | An expense higher than your recent pattern; it is not a finding of fraud. |

### Choose alerts and limits

1. Select the workspace and open **Notification settings**.
2. Review the available in-app notification types and save your preferences.
3. For a low-balance rule, select an eligible wallet and enter the amount below which you want an alert.
4. For unusual spending, select the displayed category/currency scope and minimum amount, then save the rule.
5. Review or disable existing rules as your needs change.

Unusual-spending alerts need enough past spending to compare against. No alert on a new account does not prove that a transaction is ordinary. Notifications may take a short time to appear after records change.

Bill reminder lead times are set on Bills; forecast thresholds are set in Assistant. Optional email/browser controls appear in those features where configured. Browser delivery needs permission and support on the device. You can still use the in-app list when those channels are unavailable.

## 18. Protect your account and data

### PIN and device unlock

**What this does:** Locks the app in the current browser so your figures are not left open.

1. Open **Security**.
2. Under **Confirm your identity**, enter your password and second-factor code if enabled.
3. Under **Lock this browser**, enter a new 6–8 digit PIN and choose **Set PIN**.
4. To enable your device's security method, choose **Register device security** and follow the device prompt.

Depending on your device, unlock may use a fingerprint, face scan, or device PIN. CapyBudget does not store your fingerprint or face image. Device support varies; use the offered PIN or full-login fallback if necessary.

The app can lock when backgrounded or after five minutes idle. Use its lock action when stepping away. A PIN unlocks this browser; it does not replace your account password for sensitive changes. Repeated incorrect PIN attempts can temporarily block PIN unlock, so use full authentication rather than repeatedly guessing.

### Turn on two-factor authentication

**What this does:** Adds a changing code from an authenticator app to your password when signing in.

1. Open Security and complete **Confirm your identity**.
2. Choose **Set up authenticator**.
3. Scan the displayed QR code with your authenticator app.
4. Enter its current code and choose **Verify first code to enable**.
5. Save the recovery codes somewhere safe outside this browser, then choose **I have saved these codes**.

At later sign-ins, enter the authenticator code when asked. An unused recovery code can help if you lose access to the authenticator. Recovery codes are single-use. **Replace recovery codes** creates a new set, so keep the new set instead of relying on old codes.

Email verification proves you can receive email at your address. It is separate from authenticator-based two-factor authentication.

### Review signed-in devices

1. Open Security and find **Active sessions** or **Registered browsers**.
2. Review the listed browsers and dates.
3. Use **Sign out** for one session or **Sign out other sessions** for the others.
4. Use **Remove browser** when a browser should lose both sessions and its unlock credentials.

Sensitive actions may ask for your password and second factor again. Removing the browser you are using can return you to login.

### Understand encryption

Encryption scrambles selected sensitive information and stored files so they cannot be read directly from storage. It works automatically; there is no everyday switch to turn on.

CapyBudget uses server-side field and file encryption, not end-to-end encryption. The service can read protected information when needed to run authorized features, and not every searchable field is individually encrypted.

### Hide balances on screen

Tap the eye/privacy control or open **Privacy → Hide financial amounts** and choose **Hide amounts**. Choose **Show amounts** to reveal them again.

This is useful when sharing a screen or using the app in public. It hides displayed amounts; it does not remove records or hide amounts in downloaded files.

### Download all your account data

1. Open **Privacy**.
2. Enter your password and authenticator code if enabled under **Confirm identity for data requests**.
3. Under **Export all account data**, choose **Request export**.
4. Wait for the request to show ready.
5. Choose **Download full data** and confirm your identity again if requested.

The download is a ZIP file—a package containing your personal and owned business records and retained files. Use your device's Extract or Unzip option to open it. Download availability expires after 24 hours. If the request fails or the account is too large, contact the person running the app.

This full download is different from a single PDF/Excel/CSV report.

### Backups and online sync

Changes save to the service while you are online. Other signed-in browsers refresh data when you return to them and periodically while open. Wait for a save confirmation; offline edits are not queued for later saving in this version.

Open **Privacy → Backups and online sync** to see the last verified backup and restore check. A restore check confirms that saved backup data can be recovered. If the app reports no verified backup within 26 hours, ask the person running CapyBudget to check it.

Backups are managed by the service, not by a user download button. In the current local setup, backups are stored on the same machine using SeaweedFS. They do not protect against losing that entire machine; an external destination is planned for deployment. Account export provides your own downloadable copy but is not an automatic restore/import feature.

### Delete your account

**What this does:** Starts permanent removal of your account and owned workspaces and files.

1. Download anything you want to keep first.
2. Open Privacy and complete the identity fields.
3. Choose **Review deletion** and read the list of affected workspaces and files.
4. Type **DELETE MY ACCOUNT** exactly when asked.
5. Choose **Permanently delete account** only when ready.
6. Save the private deletion receipt and use it to check cleanup status.

Access is blocked while cleanup proceeds. Already downloaded files and delivered emails cannot be recalled. Retained backups expire within the stated retention window, currently up to 35 days. This action is much broader than archiving a wallet or deleting one transaction.

## 20. A simple daily and weekly routine

### Each day

1. Check the selected workspace and Dashboard.
2. Record new spending and income with Quick add.
3. Review due bills and notifications.
4. Check Capy's forecast before an optional purchase, reading any data-limit message.

## 21. Common questions

**Why doesn't my balance match my bank?**  
Check the workspace, opening balance, missing entries, duplicates, and transaction dates. CapyBudget does not automatically import your bank activity in the MVP. Marking a bill paid alone also does not subtract an expense.

**Why can't I see Invoices?**  
Select a business workspace. On a phone, also look in More.

**Why is safe-to-spend missing?**  
Open Assistant and read Forecast limits. It may need more history, enabled data sources, or an account assigned to a bill or invoice. The app leaves the estimate unavailable rather than filling gaps with a confident-looking number.

**Does saving or confirming an action send real money?**  
No. Transactions, transfers, recurring entries, and goal contributions record activity or plans. Invoice sending and confirmed reminders do send messages when email is configured, but they do not collect a payment.

**Why did an invoice email not reach the customer?**  
Check the recipient address and the invoice's delivery history. If the app is running in the local development setup, email may be captured in its local test inbox instead of delivered externally. Ask the person running that setup to check it.

**Can I use the app without internet?**  
You need a connection to save changes. An offline message or failed request is not a successful save. Reconnect, check the existing record, and retry as needed.

**Can I connect a bank, scan a receipt automatically, or chat with Capy?**  
Those are later features. The MVP provides manual wallet tracking, receipt attachments, and the Assistant's forecast and suggestion controls. It also does not yet provide shared household access, a separate customer/vendor directory, automatic exchange rates, payroll, or inventory management.

**I only want to stop using one item. Must I delete my account?**  
No. Use the item's Archive, Pause, Skip, or Delete action as appropriate. Account deletion removes all owned financial spaces and files, not just the item currently on screen.
